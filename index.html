    National Health Mission (NHM)

    Core Vision & Architecture of NHM

    Launched initially as the National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) in 2005 and later expanded to NHM in 2013 (combining NRHM and NUHM), the mission aims to provide accessible, affordable, and quality healthcare to rural and urban vulnerable populations. It marks an architectural correction in the health system by promoting decentralized planning, communitization, and flexible financing.

    Epidemiological Targets

    • • Reduce Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) to < 25/1000 live births
    • • Reduce Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR) to < 70/100,000
    • • Reduce Total Fertility Rate (TFR) to 2.1
    • • Eradicate Leprosy, Kala-Azar & Lymphatic Filariasis

    Major Sub-Missions

    • • RMNCH+A: Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child, & Adolescent Health
    • • Health Systems Strengthening (Infrastructure & HR)
    • • National Disease Control Programmes (NDCPs)
    • • Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) Management

    Core Initiatives

    • • JSY & JSSK: Free maternal/infant transport and care
    • • RBSK: Universal child screening for 4Ds (Defects, Diseases, Deficiencies, Delays)
    • • Free Drugs & Diagnostics Service Initiatives
    • • Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (Health & Wellness Centres)

    Communitization

    Empowering communities through ASHA volunteers, Village Health Sanitation & Nutrition Committees (VHSNC), and Rogi Kalyan Samitis (RKS) for decentralized health management.

    Flexible Financing

    Provision of Untied Funds to Sub-Centres, PHCs, and CHCs, allowing local medical officers and committees to address immediate, localized healthcare infrastructure gaps.

    Program Integration

    Moving from vertical disease control to horizontal integration. Programs like NTEP (TB), NVBDCP (Malaria/Dengue), and NLEP (Leprosy) are delivered comprehensively via primary care.

    RMNCH+A Approach

    Strategic continuum of care addressing Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child, and Adolescent Health. Key drivers include JSY (cash transfers) and JSSK (free maternity services).

    National Health Programmes under NHM

    Key communicable and non-communicable disease control programs implemented horizontally via the primary healthcare system.

    NTEP

    National Tuberculosis Elimination

    Provides free diagnosis (CBNAAT/TrueNat) and DOTS treatment to eradicate TB by 2025.

    NVBDCP

    National Vector Borne Disease Control

    Controls Malaria, Dengue, Chikungunya, Kala-azar, and Lymphatic Filariasis through vector management.

    NP-NCD

    National Programme for NCDs

    Screening and management of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases, and Stroke at HWCs.

    NLEP

    National Leprosy Eradication

    Early case detection and free Multi-Drug Therapy (MDT) to reduce prevalence below 1/10,000.

    NBCP & VI

    National Blindness Control

    Free cataract surgeries, school eye screening, and spectacle distribution to reduce blindness.

    NMHP

    National Mental Health Programme

    Integration of mental healthcare with primary care, providing counseling and psychiatric drugs.

    Health Worker Cadres, Criteria & Detailed Roles

    Detailed profiles of the frontline and clinical workforce driving the NHM, based on Indian Public Health Standards (IPHS) and K. Park guidelines.

    ASHA

    Accredited Social Health Activist
    Tier 1
    Population & Criteria

    Norm: 1 per 1,000 population.
    Selection: Must be a woman resident of the village (married/widowed/divorced preferred), aged 25-45, formally educated up to at least 8th or 10th class.

    Role & Services Provided
    • Acts as a bridge between the community and the public health system.
    • Mobilizes community for ANC/PNC, immunization, and family planning.
    • Escorts pregnant women for institutional deliveries (ensuring JSY benefits).
    • Depot Holder: Distributes ORS, Iron Folic Acid (IFA), chloroquine, oral pills, and condoms at the village level.
    • Acts as a DOTS (Tuberculosis treatment) provider directly to patients.

    AWW

    Anganwadi Worker
    Tier 1
    Population & Criteria

    Norm: 1 per 400-800 population (Plains), 1 per 300-800 (Tribal).
    Selection: Local female resident, working under the ICDS (Integrated Child Development Services) scheme of WCD Ministry.

    Role & Services Provided
    • Manages the Anganwadi Centre focusing on child care and maternal nutrition.
    • Services: Provides supplementary nutrition to pregnant/lactating mothers and children under 6.
    • Conducts growth monitoring to detect Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM).
    • Provides non-formal pre-school education (ages 3-6).
    • Organizes monthly Village Health and Nutrition Days (VHNDs) alongside ANM & ASHA.

    ANM / MPHW(F)

    Auxiliary Nurse Midwife
    Tier 2
    Population & Criteria

    Norm: 1 per 5,000 population (Plains), 1 per 3,000 (Tribal).
    Selection: 10+2 pass with 2-year ANM training course, registered with the State Nursing Council.

    Role & Services Provided
    • Core frontline clinical worker at the Sub-Centre (Ayushman Arogya Mandir) level.
    • Services: Administers the Universal Immunization Programme (UIP).
    • Conducts Antenatal Care (ANC) and Postnatal Care (PNC) checkups.
    • Provides family planning services (IUD insertions, OCP distribution).
    • Handles disease surveillance, vital events registration (births/deaths), and minor ailment treatment.

    CHO / MLHP

    Community Health Officer
    Tier 2
    Population & Criteria

    Norm: 1 per Sub-Centre (Ayushman Arogya Mandir).
    Selection: B.Sc Nursing / Post Basic B.Sc Nursing / B.A.M.S. graduate with an integrated Certificate Programme in Community Health (CPCH).

    Role & Services Provided
    • Clinical lead and administrator for the primary health care team at the HWC.
    • Services: Population-based screening for Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) like Hypertension, Diabetes, and Cancers (Oral/Breast/Cervical).
    • Dispenses prescribed allopathic medicines for chronic diseases.
    • Conducts tele-consultations using the e-Sanjeevani portal with PHC/District doctors for advanced care.

    Medical Officer (MO)

    PHC Head / Civil Assistant Surgeon
    Tier 3
    Population & Criteria

    Norm: 1 per Primary Health Centre (30,000 population in plains, 20,000 in tribal/hilly areas).
    Selection: Must hold an MBBS degree and be registered with the State/National Medical Council. Acts as the medical captain of the rural health team.

    Role & Services Provided
    • Clinical head providing essential Outpatient (OPD) and Inpatient (IPD) curative care.
    • Manages basic obstetric emergencies and conducts normal institutional deliveries.
    • Supervises National Disease Control Programmes (TB, Malaria, Leprosy) at the sector level.
    • Administers the PHC, manages Untied Funds via Rogi Kalyan Samiti (RKS), and guides the CHO and ANM teams.
    • Performs minor surgeries and Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) where authorized and equipped.

    Questions about NHM

    Frequently asked questions regarding mission goals, funding, and components.

    What is the National Health Mission (NHM)?

    The National Health Mission (NHM) is an umbrella programme of the Government of India, established in 2013 under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. It incorporates the National Rural Health Mission (NRHM, 2005) and the National Urban Health Mission (NUHM, 2013) to provide universal, equitable, and high-quality healthcare services.

    What are the major components of the NHM?

    The NHM comprises four main components:

    1. NRHM: National Rural Health Mission.
    2. NUHM: National Urban Health Mission.
    3. Tertiary Care Programmes: Upgrading healthcare infrastructure.
    4. Human Resources for Health: Enhancing medical education and the healthcare workforce.

    What is the funding pattern of the NHM?

    NHM is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) with the following funding pattern between Central and State governments:
    • 60:40 for general category states.
    • 90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan States.
    • 100% central funding for Union Territories without a legislature.

    What are the main goals and targets of the NHM?
    • Reduce Maternal Mortality Rate (MMR) to 70/100,000 live births.
    • Reduce Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) to 25/1000 live births.
    • Reduce Total Fertility Rate (TFR) to 2.1.
    • Prevent and reduce anaemia in women aged 15-49 years.
    • Eradicate communicable diseases like Leprosy, Kala-Azar, and Lymphatic Filariasis.

    What are the major initiatives launched under NHM?

    Key initiatives include:
    • ASHA: Accredited Social Health Activists acting as village-level volunteers.
    • JSY & JSSK: Janani Suraksha Yojana and Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram for safe, cashless institutional deliveries.
    • Rogi Kalyan Samiti (RKS): Hospital management committees.
    • RBSK: Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram for child health screening.
    • Ayushman Arogya Mandirs: Upgraded centers providing comprehensive primary care.
